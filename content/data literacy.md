Data literacy is the ability to read, work with, analyze, and argue with data.

It's not just about being able to read charts or understand statistics. It encompasses a broader set of skills, including:
1. **Understanding Data:** Knowing what data is, where it comes from, and its potential uses and limitations.
2. **Accessing and Managing Data:** Being able to find, collect, organize, and clean data.
3. **Analyzing Data:** Using tools and techniques to explore data, identify patterns, and draw conclusions.
4. **Interpreting Data:** Making sense of the results of analysis and understanding what the data means in context.
5. **Communicating with Data:** Effectively presenting data and findings to others, often through visualizations or storytelling.
6. **Critically Evaluating Data:** Questioning the source, methodology, and potential biases of data and its presentation.
7. **Using Data Ethically:** Understanding the privacy, security, and ethical implications of working with data.

In essence, data literacy is about being comfortable and competent in a world increasingly driven by data, enabling individuals to make informed decisions and participate effectively in society and the workplace.
