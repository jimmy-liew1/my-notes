The term "Agile Scrum" is often used to emphasize that [[scrum|Scrum]] is being implemented **within an [[agile|Agile]] environment** and with a strong adherence to Agile principles and values. It is not a separate methodology distinct from either Agile or Scrum, but rather highlights Scrum's role as a specific framework for practicing Agility.

## Why the Term "Agile Scrum"? 
People may use this term to: 
- Reinforce the foundational connection between the Scrum framework and the Agile mindset. 
- Distinguish a team truly embracing Agile values through Scrum from teams merely following Scrum practices superficially ("Scrum-but"). 
- Emphasize that the goal is not just to "do Scrum" but to "be Agile" using Scrum as the vehicle. 
## How Scrum Embodies Agile Principles 
Scrum's structure and events are designed to support Agile values, such as: 
- **Individuals and Interactions:** Daily Scrum, team collaboration. 
- **Working Software:** Focus on delivering potentially shippable increments each Sprint. 
- **Customer Collaboration:** Sprint Review involves stakeholders for feedback. 
- **Responding to Change:** Sprints allow for frequent inspection and adaptation. 
- **Continuous Improvement:** Sprint Retrospective is dedicated to process improvement. 

Essentially, "Agile Scrum" serves as a reminder that successful Scrum implementation requires adopting the underlying Agile philosophy.
